# Low-input, LED-load and fault-observability review

## Result

**Qualification gaps, not a demonstrated hardware failure.** Fresh native schematic export and read-only pcbnew 9.0.2 inspection passed 36 component-value and 181 numbered-pad net comparisons. Unnumbered mechanical pads are excluded. The earlier surviving netlist has identical connectivity. Source board SHA256: `41f82c80f242eb8ded04d24c02f1ba1f39a900cd971d4bca6976d28353fcfdc5`, checkout `be51692d6b7c3069d20602cfb518370028179307`. All schematic and relevant firmware hashes are in EVIDENCE.json. No CAD or firmware was changed.

This is a logical topology/source review. It does not close the supplied board's 9 opens / 38 warnings; this review did not rerun DRC. Native net membership does not prove copper continuity.

## Verified topology and observability

- USB fuse F1 feeds VBUS_FUSED, U8 TPS259470LRPW feeds VBUS_PROTECTED_RAW, U18 TPS62932DRLR supplies +4V_REG via L2. U2 MAX38902AATA+T takes both IN and EN from +4V_REG and supplies +3V3. U7 TPS2553DBVR-1 takes +4V_REG and switches +4V_LED, powering 76 LEDs and the two U5/U6 data translators. D100 provides the labeled reverse-discharge bypass from +4V_REG toward raw protected input; its actual reverse-discharge behavior remains unqualified.
- **U8 input FLT_N is not visible to firmware:** USB_INPUT_FAULT_N contains only U8 pin 4 and R126 pin 1; R126's other side is +3V3. It is not connected to a microcontroller pin. This does not prove every low-input event would assert U8 FLT_N even if wired.
- **U12 is an isolation supervisor, not an MCU/LED reset:** TLV810EA29DBZR VDD is +3V3. RESET_HIGH reaches only R128 and U11 OE_N on GPIO_ISOLATE. It has no direct route to U1 RUN or U7 EN. RUN contains U1 pin 26, SW101 and TP6. Do not claim absence of RP2350 internal brownout protection; its configured/actual behavior was not measured here.
- LED_ENABLE connects GP22/U1 pin 34 to U7 EN with R109 100k to ground. LED_FAULT_N connects GP23/U1 pin 35 to U7 fault, TP9 and R113 10k pull-up. **This is the LED switch fault channel, not a general rail-ready/brownout detector.** U18 EN and RT remain explicitly floating, not externally programmed UVLO or a power-good signal.

## Firmware sequence and the unsupported condition

`config.h` defaults MARU60_LED_POWER_QUALIFIED to 0. A qualified build requires the settling-time and report macros. `maru_rgb.c` starts EN low, requires USB configured/transports ready/no latched fault/requested lighting, starts each enable epoch with stopped transports and a black frame, raises EN, waits the specified interval, and resumes transports. Fault/transport loss powers off; the latched LED fault is not cleared by USB reset/resume. USB deconfiguration, suspend and shutdown paths power off. These are source findings, not a newly compiled or runtime-tested result.

**An input dip that disturbs 4V/LED operation but leaves MCU execution, USB configured state, transports and U7 fault indication unchanged has no dedicated low-rail event in this source to invalidate ACTIVE and restart settling.** LED_ENABLE readback is explicitly not rail-ready. Whether any realistic waveform meets all those conditions, how LEDs behave, and whether another reset/fault actually intervenes are unknown. This is the specific qualification gap, not a demonstrated event. The default-OFF build avoids deliberately enabling this unqualified LED load, but does not qualify the buck/LDO/MCU supply itself.

## Low-input and load limits

The prior source-bound studies were inspected, not duplicated or relabeled as new active simulations:

- `continuation-97d9eb7/power-datasheet-envelope`: conditional TPS25947 UV rising 3.8626–4.1307 V, falling 3.5076–3.7747 V, using independent 0.1% resistors, electrical-table thresholds and ±0.1 µA pin leakage under its stated test conditions. These are not assembled-board guarantees. Current divider values/pins were rechecked in this review.
- `continuation-fa96a7c/power-audit`: U18 recommended VIN minimum 3.8 V is not a promise of regulated 4.0 V. A buck cannot boost input below output, and conductor/dropout losses worsen headroom. Loss of 4V regulation alone does not establish 3V3 failure. MAX38902A's cited 100 mV dropout maximum is at defined conditions and a 95%-nominal output criterion, not a system brownout threshold.
- Current native values retain 5.8 µF nominal raw-input bypass and 49.7 µF directly on +4V_REG. TI's typical 10 µF input recommendation is a review flag, not a mandatory-minimum violation. Switched LED capacitance is a startup load, not automatically available direct buck output capacitance. “effective>=12uF TBD” on C140/C141 is an unverified requirement. L2 and C100 already have candidate MPN evidence; they must not be described as wholly unspecified.
- USB_MAX_POWER_CONSUMPTION=500 is a descriptor, not measured or enforced supply current. Brightness 64 and PWM sum limit 1020 constrain numeric output, not instantaneous or worst-case electrical current. 76 LED idle currents, PWM coincidence, selected LED variants, translator consumption, MCU activity, LDO current and buck/eFuse losses must be included. No invented per-LED current or efficiency was used.

For an eventual budget, use measured/guaranteed input constraints and loss terms: `Pavailable = Vsource * Iallowed`; require `P4V/eta_buck + Pinput_losses <= Pavailable`, with `P4V` including LED/translator consumption, LDO input current and all direct 4V loads. Also verify peak and time-window source current independently; a passing average-power inequality cannot establish inrush/PWM/current-limit compliance. Neither an actual safe LED budget nor a voltage floor is established here.

## Minimal qualification plan and necessary inputs

1. **Define limits first:** minimum/maximum source voltage at the connector and U18, cable/connector/fuse resistance and source current-limit/recovery behavior; permitted 4V/3V3 excursions/durations; MCU BOR configuration; required LED undervoltage and data-state behavior. Obtain adopted LED part current/voltage limits, full MCU/USB/flash load profiles, allowed expansion loads and operating temperatures.
2. **Effective passive data:** selected capacitor DC-bias/temp/tolerance/age capacitance and ESR/ESL, inductor L(I,T)/DCR, protection thresholds/current limits/delays, and thermal limits. Preserve existing C100/L2 candidate evidence. Do not arbitrarily increase capacitance or replace UVLO resistors without these inputs.
3. **Instrument both sides of protection:** source/connector VBUS, VBUS_FUSED, VBUS_PROTECTED_RAW/U18 VIN, 4V_REG, 3V3, 4V_LED, input/inductor current, USB_INPUT_FAULT_N, GPIO_ISOLATE, RUN, LED_ENABLE, LED_FAULT_N and both LED data chains. Correlate firmware configured/state/transport logs with voltage traces. Testpoint availability/probe loading must be planned, especially the input fault net without a dedicated listed TP.
4. **Sweep distinct states:** default LEDs OFF; controlled qualified startup/black; maximum allowed sustained pattern and worst instantaneous PWM/load transition; USB configured versus suspend/deconfigured. Test cold/hot start, prebias, slow fall/rise through the U8 threshold region, fast shallow/deep dips, repeated dips/replug, load-step/short-release and recovery. Choose dip depths/durations from actual component thresholds and measured time constants rather than invented universal pass points. Include the key case where 4V droops but MCU/USB remain alive.
5. **Pass criteria:** no exceeded absolute limits or unqualified operating intervals, acceptable rails/current/temperature, no unintended data/backfeed, deterministic reset/off behavior and settling after every relevant rail recovery. If the low-rail blind spot is demonstrated, choose between explicit rail-good/reset gating, adding meaningful monitoring, or a documented qualified ride-through envelope. Do not wire U8 fault blindly: its event coverage/timing must first be verified.
6. **Model only after nominal validation:** reproduce a supported complete vendor simulator bench, validate nominal regulation/startup, then insert the real source/protection/load/passive data and compare with measurements. Existing TPS62932/TPS2553 XSPICE-wrapper failures remain blocked; no rerun or pass is claimed. Passive charging studies cannot predict active regulation or loop stability. Where no supported model exists, retain the gap and measure hardware.

## Targeted current-limit supplement

**Protection setting is not a 500 mA USB compliance guarantee.** Native R124 is 6.65 kΩ ±0.1%, U8 ILM-to-GND. [TI TPS25947 Rev C](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps25947.pdf), pp8–9,32, gives R=3334/I, hence 501.353 mA nominal equation estimate; the exact 6.65 kΩ table row is 425/500/575 mA. Its conditions include VIN=12 V and TJ=−40…125°C, not an assembled 5-V guarantee. Do not substitute the >1-A accuracy claim. Recommended R range is 549–6650 Ω, without an explicit nominal-versus-actual tolerance qualifier. The +0.1% endpoint is 6656.65 Ω numerically, but this alone does not establish a design violation. No resistor-only adjustment proves a 500-mA ceiling. Open ITIMER minimizes delay, not instantaneous clamping; startup foldback and transient response still matter.

Native R112 is 133 kΩ ±1%, U7 ILIM-to-GND. [TI TPS2553-1 Rev G](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2553-1.pdf), pp5–7,16–18, gives min/nom/max equations: 25230/R^1.016, 23950/R^0.977, 22980/R^0.94 (mA, R in kΩ). Including resistor endpoints yields **173.657/201.513/233.901 mA**; equations include process/temperature variation but require external resistor tolerance separately. Both endpoints satisfy the recommended 15–232 kΩ range. Electrical-table conditions are recommended operation, EN at 0/VIN, RFAULT=10 kΩ; the 133-kΩ result is equation-derived, not its own tabulated production-test row. TPS2553-1 limits initially then latches off for sustained overcurrent after deglitch, rather than continuously supplying the threshold indefinitely.

The [TI TPS25947EVM guide](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/slvuc01/slvuc01.pdf), SLVUC01 September 2020, schematic p5 and BOM p23, uses 6.65 kΩ ±1% R7/R20 (Yageo RC0603FR-076K65L) for selectable current settings. This directly supports nominal 6.65-kΩ selection with ordinary tolerance and counters treating the rounded table endpoint as an automatic failure. The precise table-bound tolerance interpretation is not explicit; no resistor replacement is justified by this arithmetic.

The firmware's 500 mA descriptor neither measures nor enforces input current. U8's table spread straddles that number. U7 protects only the switched LED/translator branch after the buck; its current cannot be subtracted directly from a USB-input current budget. Actual other loads, conversion losses and transients are still unknown. A protection threshold above 500 mA is not by itself USB noncompliance or normal consumption. These branch-threshold estimates do not bound instantaneous waveforms. No actual overdraw or nuisance trip is established. No resistor/firmware change was made. The prior passive deck omitted R124 and current limiting; these calculations do not repair that model or demonstrate active behavior.
